Project 1: FirstBank “Giant In You” Campaign

The problem

FirstBank wanted to know which customers say yes to its term deposit campaign, so it could target them better.

How I solved it

The problem

GT Bank was losing one in five customers and needed to know who leaves and why, in order to improve retention.

How I solved it

Removed personal data (surname, customer ID), standardised the fields, kept valid zero-balance accounts, then compared churn by age, gender, activity, tenure, products and balance.

Project 5: Nigerian Fish Store Sales

Retail, profitability and suppliers
Click to open the full report (PDF)
22.4MNGN total sales across 1,826 records
16.2%profit margin (NGN 3.63M profit)
+19.4%sales growth 2019 to 2023
17.6% to 15.4%margin decline over the same period
The problem

A fish distribution business wanted to grow profit and needed to know its popular products, profit drivers, suppliers and customers.

How I solved it

Unified 1,826 mixed-format dates, removed a duplicate column, excluded 527 unreliable restock dates and calculated discount cost separately, since sales value ignored discounts.

Key findings
  • Catfish (3,958 kg) and the 1 kg size (4,051 kg) sell most; Tilapia leads in Port Harcourt.
  • Sales grew but profit stayed flat because margin fell.
  • Discounts add no extra volume; applied per unit they cost NGN 1.27M, 35% of gross profit.

The problem

Road safety planners needed to know the highest-risk places, times and causes of crashes across Nigeria.

How I solved it

Renamed cryptic column codes, split quarters into year and quarter, set aside two unreliable columns, showed overlapping causes as shares, and compared regions by deaths per 100 crashes.

Project 7: Port Harcourt Ride Data

Transport, usage and anomaly detection
Click to open the full report (PDF)
1,155rides analysed, 12,205 miles
93%of rides are business
48%of rides start between 13:00 and 18:00
42%of all miles from just 77 long trips
The problem

A ride-hailing business wanted to understand travel patterns, separate business from personal use and cut unnecessary mileage.

How I solved it

Removed a totals row, converted mixed dates (421 Excel numbers and 734 text dates), flagged 13 impossible durations and 15 impossible speeds, then analysed rides by hour, day, purpose and trip length.

Key findings
  • Rides peak at 15:00 and Monday is the busiest day.
  • 16 rides over 100 miles make up 22% of mileage, so reviewing long trips is the quickest way to cut cost.
  • 43% of rides have no recorded purpose and 13 have impossible durations, which I flagged.

Project 9: UBA Bank Transaction Fraud Detection

Banking, fraud detection
Click to open the full report (PDF)
200,000transactions analysed
10,088fraud cases identified
5.04%fraud rate
NGN 497Mvalue of fraud
The problem

UBA needed to understand fraud patterns across transactions and identify behavioural or operational risk indicators that could improve fraud monitoring.

How I solved it

Cleaned and standardised 200,000 transactions, removed personal data, converted date and time fields, created time-based variables, and analysed fraud by account type, transaction type, device, merchant, location, amount, balance and customer age.

Key findings
  • Fraud accounted for 5.04% of transactions, with fraud rates remaining between 4.4% and 5.5% across major segments.
  • Business accounts and transfers had slightly higher fraud rates at 5.17% and 5.19%.
  • Debit/Credit Card and Virtual Card transactions recorded the highest device-related fraud rates at 5.50% and 5.45%.
  • No single factor clearly separated fraudulent from legitimate transactions, highlighting the need for broad monitoring and richer data.

Project 10: Nigerian Digital Payments Fraud Analysis

Fraud & risk, digital payments
Click to open the full report (PDF)
50,000transactions after cleaning
2,460fraud cases identified
4.92%fraud rate
NGN 726Mvalue of fraud
The problem

A Nigerian digital payment platform needed to identify behavioural and operational risk factors linked to fraud and improve its fraud monitoring approach.

How I solved it

Removed 1,000 duplicate transaction IDs, standardised the data, retained and labelled missing values, rounded inconsistent amounts, and analysed fraud by transaction type, amount, hour, account age, past fraud, recent activity, device, payment method and location.

Key findings
  • Fraud accounted for 4.92% of 50,000 transactions and represented NGN 726M, or 5.1% of total transaction value.
  • Fraud looked similar to legitimate activity, with no transaction type, amount band or hour showing a strong pattern.
  • Transactions with missing device records had a 6.12% fraud rate compared with 4.86% for the rest.
  • Account age, previous fraud history and recent activity did not show a meaningful relationship with fraud.
